# AGENTS.md — MyPersonalWeb

Guide for any AI agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI…) working in this repo from scratch. Read it first, then the files it points to. This repo is **public**: never add private or job-search data here (that lives in a separate private repo).

## What this is
Álvaro Rodríguez Molina's portfolio, deployed at https://alvrich.vercel.app. React + TypeScript + SCSS, built with Vite and **prerendered** so pages read without JavaScript. Eleven UI languages, light/dark themes, eight accent colours.

## Layout
- `src/components/` — header, mobile menu, pickers, icons.
- `src/sections/` — page sections (Hero, Experience, Education, Work, Skills, Contact, GitHub activity, Radar).
- `src/data/` — **content**: `experience.ts` (jobs and roles), `work.ts` (projects), `contact.ts`.
- `src/i18n/locales/*.json` — eleven translation files. Any user-visible text must be added to **all eleven** (`en` is the reference).
- `src/styles/partials/` — SCSS by section. `_mobile.scss` and `_responsive.scss` hold the phone layout (≤760px).
- `game.html` + `src/game/` — **Bug Hunter**, an Archero-style canvas game with Survivor.io-style weapons (move to dodge, stand still and ranged weapons fire; melee and magic work all the time; bugs drop XP gems; twelve rooms: ten fights with bosses in rooms 6 and 12, plus two shrine rooms (5 and 11); each level up offers one melee, one magic and one ranged or upgrade pick). It has two modes (`Mode` in `world.ts`, `save.mode`, picked on the cover's pill under the play bar or on the map screen's `.mode-pick`): **Sprints** is that room game; **On call** («Guardia», `'horde'`) is the Survivor.io take: `newGame(…, 'horde')` calls `startField()` instead of `enterRoom()`, the hero starts at (0, 0) on an open field with no walls (`keepInRoom` only clamps in sprints, `outside()` is never true there, shots and orbs die by their `life`; Ricochet arrows turn towards the nearest bug not yet hit, in both modes, so the upgrade still means something there), the camera rides on the hero (`draw()` centres the transform on `view.cx/cy`, which `resize()` sets to the middle of the area under the HUD; `drawField()` tiles the plain middle of the world's floor picture, `FLOOR_CROP`, mirrored tile by tile; fog and the retro grid cover `vis`, the visible rect) and `updateHorde()` runs the ten minutes (`HORDE_LENGTH`): spawns on a ring just past the visible edge (`ringSpot`, from `g.viewHalf`, which the page sets every frame from the canvas), faster and in bigger groups with the clock, tickets from 1:30 and legacy from 2:30 with a cap on tickets and on everyone (`cap`), toughness and speed scaling with the minute instead of the room, and each world past the first pouring faster (7% per world), in bigger groups, with a higher cap and more frequent hazard patches, stragglers far behind teleported back to the ring, hazard patches (lava, puddles, thorns) dropped near you now and then, a coin bonus and a `'minute'` event each minute, bosses summoned at `HORDE_BOSS_AT` (5:00 and 10:00; boss HP there is its own, `bossHp`; the second is `final` for its attacks; backups only while there is room), and the win when the last boss dies. There tougher bugs drop health hearts (`g.hearts`, `HEART_CHANCE`: tickets 6%, legacy 35%, bosses always three; each heals `HEART_HEAL` = 12% of max health, is drawn in when you pass within 60, and fades after `HEART_LIFE` seconds, blinking at the end; `drawHearts` in `draw.ts`). There, ranged weapons also fire while moving at 1/1.8 of the rate (standing still is still better), lightning strikes come slower, and levels climb geometrically (`xpNext × 1.22`) because XP pours in. Records per world in `save.hordeBest` (time, kills, won). Each mode has its own world progress: sprints use `save.unlocked`/`save.world`, on call `save.hordeUnlocked`/`save.hordeWorld` (`unlockedIn()`, `worldIn()`, `setWorld()` in `main.ts`; the cover and the map show the current mode's), and winning a world unlocks the next only in that mode. Balance was checked with a kiting bot (`scratchpad` sims): a careless bot lasts about three minutes in world 1, so a person should get much further; tune `updateHorde` if the owner finds it too easy or too hard. **Evolutions** (after Survivor.io's EVO, `EVO_PARTNER` in `world.ts`): a weapon at its top level plus one particular upgrade at level 1 or more (sword+Refactor, blades+Ricochet, hammer+Heart, storm+Chain lightning, fireball+Fire arrows, frost+Water arrows, spirits+Ghost arrows, knives+Pair programming, boomerang+Leaf fan) makes `canEvolve` true, and the next level up offers it as a gold `evo:<weapon>` card (`Choice`, `evoOf`; it takes that weapon's category slot); choosing it sets `g.evolved[weapon]` and each weapon's function reads that flag for its stronger form (Lunar Halo: full-circle slash; Orbital Saw: +2 saws; Seismic Hammer: wider, stuns; Supercell: double bolts, faster, chains; Inferno Bomb: three fireballs, `g.embers` burning ground; Blizzard: wider, near-freeze; Spectral Legion: +2 spirits, faster, pierce; Knife Rain: a ring of piercing knives; Quantum Boomerang: +2, faster); the HUD column, the skills list and the cards show a gold ring and `★ EVO`, and the list says what each weapon evolves with (`evoWith`). The spinning blades go up to level 5 (`bladeCount`, `bladeScale`, `bladeOrbit` in `world.ts`: 2, 3, 4, 4, 5 blades; the circle widens every level, to 1.8× at level 5, and the saws grow from level 3, to 1.9× at 5; the hit reach is the saw's own radius, and the level-up card says so; they also orbit 8% faster per level, and `drawBlades` adds motion that grows with the level: a fading streak along the circle from level 2, longer each level, and echo copies behind each saw from level 3) and take an element from the other skills you carry (`bladeElement` in `world.ts`, the one with most levels wins: fire from fire arrows or the fireball burns, ice from frost or water arrows slows, volt from lightning arrows, sparks or the storm arcs to one more bug, shade from ghost arrows cuts 30% deeper; the Spirits skill counts for the worn spirit's element, `SPIRIT_BLADE`: the water ghost and Tide for ice, Ember for fire, Volt for volt, Shade for shade, Thorn for none, plain steel with none): `drawBlades` draws the owner's saw blade spinning as it orbits (`static/images/game/blades/<element>.webp`: `steel` and `volt` are the owner's own pictures, fire, ice and shade recolour only the steel one's red parts; glow from `BLADE_LOOK`; retro keeps the drawn blades), and the level-up card and HUD show that element's medallion (`skills/blades-{fire,ice,volt,shade}.webp`, `iconFile()` in `main.ts`) with its own name and text. The Spirits skill is a companion: one ghost per level floats at the hero's side (`SPIRIT_SLOTS`) and keeps shooting an `orb` shot at the nearest bug, even while you move; it is drawn from the owner's painted ghost (`static/images/game/spirits/<skin>-float-0..4.webp` and `-shoot-{l,r}.webp`, whole square canvases drawn centred; `drawSpirits` in `draw.ts`, the throwing pose shows for `cast` seconds after each shot, facing `dir`): the companion's look and element come from the loadout (`spirit: SpiritSkin` in `shop.ts`): `water` is the friendly default (five floating frames and a throw each way), and five element spirits are bought for the magic slot (the owner's sets in folders fire, water, leaf, bolt, shadow map to `fire`, `tide`, `thorn`, `volt`, `shade`; every set has the same seven frames, all scaled so the body matches the water ghost's size, `SPIRIT_FRAMES`), each with an orb effect in `hitWithShot` (fire burns, tide slows, thorn pierces one bug, volt arcs to one more, shade passes through all); orb colours in `SPIRIT_COLOR`; retro keeps the hand-drawn ghost and the purple orb. Eight worlds of twelve sprints (`WORLDS` in `world.ts`): world 1 is the plain backlog, worlds 2–8 are themed by element with their own colours, a hazard (lava, puddles, thorns, lightning, bouncing shots, fog, mixed) and tougher bugs; finishing a world unlocks the next on the world map (`mapScreen` in `main.ts`, progress in `save.ts`). The map is built from the owner's island artwork in `static/images/game/map/` (one WebP per world, plus a 16:10 preview each; `rayo` and `nucleo` are hue-shifted copies of `infierno` and `demonios`): `ISLAND_META` holds, per image, where the ring, name plate and badge sit so the translated overlays line up with the art. The player has a name (`save.name`, default `alvrich`, cleaned by `cleanName` in `save.ts`, at most `NAME_MAX` characters, always escaped with `esc()` before it goes into HTML): it shows on the cover's profile card and under the hero in Customise, and the profile card opens `profileScreen` (name field, level, runs, worlds, coins; the window key handler ignores keys typed into fields). The boy skin is called Alvrich. **Masteries**: once all eight worlds of a mode are cleared (`clearedIn`/`worldsLeft` in `main.ts`; a sprints world counts once won, an on-call one once its shift is won), the map offers that mode's mastery (`masteryScreen`); taking it adds one to `save.mastery[mode]` and calls `wipeProgress` (`save.ts`: worlds of both modes, records, coins, items, forge, level and runs go; name, skin, settings, masteries and `starColor` stay), and every mastery adds for good `MASTERY_STEP` (+5% damage, +10 health, +5% coins and XP, via `buildLoadout`'s third argument). Stars in the chosen colour (`STAR_COLORS`) show by the name on the cover and the profile; the profile also picks the colour and has a reset button (`resetScreen`, which first says how many worlds are left for each mastery) that calls the same `wipeProgress`. It has a main menu with a shop (`shop.ts`: coins earned per run buy items worn in six slots, head, body, boots, magic, melee, ranged (`SLOTS`; older saves had `charm` and `relic`, `loadSave()` re-seats those items by their current slot), and the **forge**, seven permanent upgrades bought level by level, `UPGRADES`/`upgradePrice`, kept in `save.upgrades` and folded into the run's loadout by `buildLoadout(equipped, upgrades)` whatever is worn: vitality, might, armour, swiftness, magnet, fortune, wisdom; the loadout's `hasteMult`, `magnet` and `armor` are read in `world.ts`), a **Customise** screen (`stylesScreen` in `main.ts`, opened by the cover's third card and laid out after the owner's mock-up: the title in the gem plate (`ui/title_plate.webp`), the hero standing on the glowing pad (`ui/glow_pad.webp`, floor ring at 75% of its height), three slot cards down each side, armour left (head, body, boots) and weapons right (magic, melee, ranged), each one of the owner's rings sitting on a chamfered CSS plate with the slot name and the worn item (`ui/ring_{grey,blue,orange_on}.webp` for empty, worn and open), and three round buttons under it, back (`btn_prev`), character and enemies; tapping a slot or one of the two buttons lists what fits there in ring-and-plate rows, `ui/row2_*.webp`, ring centre at 20.3% and plate 38–91% of the width, measured on the picture; one tap wears an owned item or buys and wears it; below it the skin and the enemy look pickers: original, demon, Greek arena, nightmare, random, drawn in `draw.ts`; in retro the hero preview is a canvas drawn by `drawRetroFigure()`, which also shows the body item as a cape and the boots as feet, the painted sprite only knows the hat), and sound options; the dialogs, HUD, cards and crests are cut from the owner's UI sheets into `static/images/game/ui/` (the two stone-and-neon frames, squat and tall, are drawn whole behind each dialog, never sliced: `fitPanel()` in `main.ts` measures the content, picks the frame whose shape is closer and sets height and padding from the bands in `FRAMES`, so the content sits in the plain centre and long dialogs scroll inside; `show()` lifts the `.ui-close` button out of the scrolling box so it stays put, and a dialog shown with `'keep-scroll'` (the shop) keeps its scroll position across re-renders (`fitPanel()` reapplies it); the frame pictures are padded so the gem's axis is the exact centre of the file, so never recentre them with a transform; the card WebPs are cut without the dark matte the sheets carry around each piece, so no CSS masks are needed; every UI picture's visual centre is within 1px of its file's centre (measure the alpha centroid and the mid-row extent before adding one; `btn2_green` was rebuilt by mirroring its intact half because the sheet cut its left end); row cards place the text column exactly on the picture's body (ring 5–29%, body 29–91% of the width: columns `31.87% 1fr` with `padding-right: 9%`, since grid percentages resolve against the content box) and the icon on the ring's centre at 17%; side-by-side button rows are stacked everywhere because two bevelled bars are wider than the frame's plain centre and would shift the whole column; the owner's three chamfered bars as `border-image` (`ui/btn2_{blue,green,orange}.webp`: blue for the main action, green to play, orange for anything that costs coins; grey `btn_gray` for secondary buttons); crests for pause, level-up, shrine, victory, defeat and boss with the title set underneath; the level-up picks use the tall card pictures on wide screens (`tall_*.webp`, type sized in `cqw` so the longest description fits inside the body plate at any card width) and plain outlined rows on phones; the enemy-look picker uses the row cards; see `.ui-head`, `.panel .skill`, `.panel .style` in `game.css`); the health (green, with its own heart), XP (purple) and boss (red, spiked) meters come from the owner's HUD sheet, each split into an empty frame and a fill (`ui/meter2_{hp,xp,boss}_{frame,fill}.webp`; in a boss fight no crest is shown, the name sits in the horned plate (`ui/boss_plate.webp`) over the bar just under the HUD, and `resize()` starts the room right under the HUD (below the boss bar in a boss fight) and hugs it to the top, with the weapon badges as a column down the right edge over the playfield (`.gear`) so no row steals height from the room; `g.bladePick` wins over the automatic choice while still available) and the spirit (`pickSpirit`: the water ghost and every spirit you own); a tap outside, the ✕, Esc or P closes it and the run goes on; Each world has a painted arena floor (`static/images/game/floors/worldN.webp`, drawn under walls, racks and hazards by `drawRoom`; flat tiles in the retro look). The retro look uses the hand-drawn figures. Sheets are cut with margins on every side and per-cell connected components, so parts beyond the grid line survive and neighbours don't; the Jarl's strike and hit frames overlap on the sheet beyond repair, so `viking-boss-4/5.webp` are copies of its wind-up and base frames. The fireball, its blast (two frames, small then big), the throwing knife, the boomerang, the sword's slash (`slash`, the one whole crescent of the three on the sheet, split from its neighbours by nearest core; turned to the swing and grown in code) and the storm's strike (`bolt-1..5` cycling then `bolt-6`, the ground burst; the long sky zigzag is skipped when the picture is in) are painted pieces cut from the owner's effects sheet into `static/images/game/fx/` (cut from the owner's clean sheet, whose alpha is soft everywhere: pixels under alpha 35 are dropped and the rest rescaled, and glow from neighbouring pieces is kept only near each piece's own core, `scratchpad` `fx2/cutclean.py`; `fxImage()` in `draw.ts`, all pointing right; retro keeps the drawn shapes). ## Commands
- `npm install`, then `npm run dev` (Vite dev server).
- `npm run typecheck` — TypeScript check. **Run before committing.**
- `npm run build` — full build with prerender. Also rewrites `static/data/github-contributions.json` from the GitHub API; if you did not mean to change it, `git checkout -- static/data/github-contributions.json` before committing.
- There is no test suite (`npm test` is a placeholder). Verify UI changes in a browser: serve with `npx vite preview --port 4179`, then take screenshots with Playwright. In this environment use the installed headless shell `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium_headless_shell-1194/chrome-linux/headless_shell` as `executablePath`; do **not** run `playwright install`.

## Working rules
- **Mobile first check.** Anything visual must be checked at 360–390px wide as well as desktop.
- **Edit content in data files, not in components**, when the change is a fact (dates, titles, projects). Components only render.
- **Dates:** `YYYY-MM` strings. Keep them consistent with the CV (see the CV section below).
- **Git:** commit as the configured user; **no `Co-Authored-By` or AI attribution lines** unless the owner asks. Push to `main` only when asked. Pull (`--ff-only`) before you work; never force-push `main`.
- Keep commits small and in English conventional style (`feat(experience): …`, `fix(mobile): …`).

## The CV (`scripts/cv/`)
- The CV is an HTML template (`cv.html`) that reproduces the original Canva layout in Open Sans, A4, two pages. Edit text there, then `node scripts/cv/build.mjs` (needs Playwright; set `CHROMIUM_PATH` if the default browser is missing).
- Keep the CV and the website in agreement: same titles, same dates, same company names. The website's `src/data/experience.ts` is the reference for the public record; after changing it, update the CV too.
- Check the result visually: two pages, A4, no text overflowing a column, bottom margin roughly even on both pages.

## Portrait easter egg
- A click on the hero portrait spins it like a coin and sets off the current accent's element: blue water ripples, green leaves blown left, red flames round the ring, orange fireworks (streaks that fall as dots), yellow lightning, white ghosts, pink hearts orbiting, and confetti under Disco. Quick repeat clicks build a combo (more pieces, further).
- Code: `src/sections/HeroAbout.tsx` (click, combo, piece lifetime), `src/components/portraitFx.ts` (which effect per accent, where each piece starts and goes) and `src/styles/partials/_portrait-fx.scss` (shapes and animations). Keep the durations in `portraitFx.ts` (`LIFE`) in step with the SCSS.
- It is hidden on purpose: default cursor, no button role. The hints are one line in `src/i18n/bubble.json` (desktop, after the CV line) and a single wiggle of the portrait on touch screens if nobody taps it within 4 s of loading the page or of changing the accent.
- Rules it must keep: nothing runs with `prefers-reduced-motion`; on phones (≤760px) no piece may leave the screen sideways (check `document.documentElement.scrollWidth` while it plays); every piece is removed after its burst.

## Things that caused bugs before
- Menus and pickers on phones must fit inside the screen (they were once offset to the right).
- Background decoration in the CV must not overlap text; the bottom decoration is mirrored on purpose.
- Adding a new `.xp-…` element without the matching SCSS leaves it unstyled; check the section after every change.
